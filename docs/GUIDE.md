# Student guide

For exchange students choosing courses at UC3M's School of Engineering (Leganés campus).
Open **https://polluxgnr.github.io/uc3m-course-planner/**. There's nothing to install and no account, and
your choices are saved in your own browser.

## The fast way: "Plan it for me"

Seven quick questions:

- what you're into
- maths
- how you like to be graded
- mornings
- long weekends
- how packed the week can be
- teaching language

A counter shows how many courses are still in the running as you answer. Then you get a drafted semester:
four **Want** picks and six **Backup** picks, each with the reason it was chosen ("no final exam", "barely
any maths", "deep in AI & software"…).

- **Swap the Want picks:** get alternatives instead.
- **Change answers:** go back to the questions.
- **Build my timetable:** let the optimiser turn the draft into real plans.

## The chatty way: the AI advisor

Click **Advisor** and talk to it like a friend who has read every syllabus:

- "Build me a 30 ECTS plan focused on AI, light on maths, Fridays free."
- "What will I actually learn in Mobile Applications? Is it useful for a data job?"
- "Machine Learning or Artificial Intelligence?"

It searches the catalog, reads course details, edits your shortlist and settings, and always runs the
optimiser before recommending a plan, so its suggestions never clash. Course names in its answers are
clickable.

**Getting a key:**
- The advisor needs an OpenRouter key: sign up at openrouter.ai, create a key, and set a small spending
  limit. A whole conversation typically costs a few cents.
- Your key stays in your browser and is only sent to openrouter.ai.
- **Forget my key** in the advisor's settings removes it. Don't save it on a shared computer.

## The hands-on way

### 1. Explore

- **Course map:** each dot is a course, placed by maths load (left is lighter) and hands-on share (up is
  more labs and projects). Colour shows AI and software focus. Hover a dot for a summary card, click it to
  open the course.
- **Catalog:** search topics, tools or course names in English or Spanish ("python", "android",
  "robots"). Filter by degree, year and language, or use the quick filters: Maths ≤ 3, Final exam ≤ 50%,
  Fits my plan.
- **Match score:** the number on each row is how well that course alone fits your current weights.

### 2. Open a course

- **What it is:** the AI summary (what you'll learn, what it's useful for, tools, a heads-up), plus the
  official objectives and contents.
- **How it's graded:** the split between final exam and continuous work, and deadline weeks.
- **When it meets:** every group with its days, times, language and course code.
- **Compare:** add up to three courses for a side-by-side table.

### 3. Shortlist

| Mark | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Must** | Always in the plan (say, your home university requires it). |
| **Want** | Preferred. "Your picks" rewards plans that include more of them. |
| **Backup** | Fine if needed. Used to complete a plan or stand in for a full course. |

Aim for 40–60 ECTS so the optimiser has room.

**Heat map:** under your list, "When your shortlist meets" shows when your courses could be in class.
Pale columns are where a free day can come from.

### 4. Say what matters

Nine sliders from 0 (ignore) to 5 (very important). Four describe the courses: AI & software, hands-on,
maths load, continuous assessment. Three describe the week: compact week, contact hours, no dead time.
Then fewer courses, and your picks.

Presets: **Balanced**, **Interest first**, **Compact week**. Hard limits sit underneath: target and maximum
ECTS, earliest start, latest end, days to keep free.

### 5. Read the plans

You get the three best plans (A, B, C):

- **Score and breakdown:** each bar shows how close the plan gets to the best possible, next to the raw
  number.
- **Plan profiles:** a radar chart of all three plans. Further out is better; click a shape to switch plans.
- **Compare:** the best value in each row is highlighted.
- **Courses and fallbacks:** for each course, other groups and replacement courses, ranked by the plan
  score after the swap.
- **Week:** solid blocks are weekly lectures, hatched blocks are labs that run only some weeks. Tick
  **Other groups** to see alternatives.
- **Semester:** class hours per week, lab weeks, deadlines (◆), the break, and each course's final-exam
  share.

The status line says whether the search was **exhaustive**, meaning these really are the best plans for
your settings.

### 6. Learning Agreement

**Copy plan** puts the plan on your clipboard: each course's name, ECTS, **course code**, group, sessions and
top fallback.

## Good to know

- **Course codes differ by degree.** Use the code of the group you picked.
- **Language per group isn't published.** A course tagged EN has an English option; confirm which group is
  the English one.
- **Exam dates weren't published** when the data was fetched. The header shows the exam period.
- **Scores and AI summaries are estimates.** Use them to narrow down, then read the syllabus.
- **Timetables can change** before the semester. Check the official site before you enrol.

Found a bug or a wrong score? Open an issue on the repository.

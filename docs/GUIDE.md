# Student guide

A walkthrough for exchange students choosing courses at UC3M's School of Engineering (EPS, Leganés
campus). Nothing to install: open the planner link in a browser. Your choices are saved in your browser.

## 1. Look around: the course map

Each dot is a course, placed by **maths load** (left = lighter) and **hands-on share** (up = more labs,
projects and continuous work). Colour shows the **AI and software focus**. Dashed rings mark courses
recommended by past exchange students. Click any dot to open the course.

## 2. Filter the catalog

- **Language:** "Taught in English" by default. It also restricts the optimiser to groups of
  English-taught degree variants.
- **Degree** and **year:** you can mix degrees freely as long as your home university agrees.
- **Quick filters:** Maths ≤ 3, Final exam ≤ 50%, and "Fits the current plan", which shows only courses that
  would slot into the plan you're looking at without a clash.
- **Search** covers names in English and Spanish, course codes and syllabus contents (try "python",
  "android", "neural").

Every row shows a **match** score, which is how well that course alone fits your weights, plus its ECTS,
estimated class hours, final-exam share, year and degrees.

## 3. Open a course

The side panel shows what the official syllabus says:

- **About and contents:** what the course covers.
- **Assumed knowledge:** the courses you're expected to have taken.
- **Assessment:** the split between final exam and continuous work, plus deadlines the syllabus mentions
  by week.
- **Groups:** every teaching group with its times, language and course code.

Follow **Official syllabus** for the full text.

## 4. Build a shortlist

Mark courses with:

| Mark | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Must** | Always in the plan (for example, required by your home university). |
| **Want** | Preferred. The "Your picks" weight rewards plans that include more of them. |
| **Backup** | Fine if needed. Used to complete a plan or to replace a course that's full. |

Shortlist more than 30 ECTS (40–60 is comfortable) so the planner has room to work and to find fallbacks.
If "Let the optimiser suggest courses" is on, it also tries the 18 best-matching courses you didn't
shortlist. Transversal skills courses are never suggested.

## 5. Tell it what matters

Nine sliders from 0 (ignore) to 5 (very important):

- **AI & software, Hands-on, Maths load, Continuous assessment:** what the courses are like.
- **Compact week, Contact hours, No dead time:** what your timetable is like.
- **Fewer courses:** reaching 30 ECTS with five 6-ECTS courses rather than eight small ones.
- **Your picks:** how much your Want marks count.

Presets: **Balanced**, **Interest first** (content over timetable) and **Compact timetable**.
Hard limits sit under the sliders:

- **ECTS target and maximum** (top right).
- **Earliest start and latest end.**
- **Keep these days free.**

## 6. Read the plans

The planner shows the three best plans, Plan A, B and C, each with:

- **Score and breakdown:** each criterion's bar shows how close the plan gets to the best possible, next to
  the raw number (hours per week, free weekdays, final-exam share…).
- **Compare plans:** the best value in each row is highlighted.
- **Fallback coverage:** for how many courses there's a backup that keeps the plan complete and close in
  score.
- **Week view:** solid blocks are weekly lectures and hatched blocks are labs that run only some weeks.
  Tick "Show other groups" to see alternatives.
- **Semester view:** class hours per week, lab weeks, syllabus deadlines (◆), the break and the
  final-exam share per course.

The line under the tabs says whether the search was **exhaustive** (these really are the best plans for
your settings) or hit its time limit.

## 7. Fill in your Learning Agreement

**Copy plan** puts the plan on your clipboard as text, with each course's name, ECTS, **course code**,
group, timetable and its top fallback. Paste it into your notes or send it to your coordinator.

## Good to know

- **Course codes differ by degree.** The same course can have a different code in each degree that teaches
  it. The planner merges them and shows the code of the group you picked. Use that one.
- **Language per group isn't published.** A course tagged EN has an English option. Check which group is
  the English one in the syllabus or with the International Office.
- **Exam dates aren't in the data yet.** The ordinary exam period and the resits are in the header.
- **The scores are estimates** read from syllabus text. Use them to narrow down, then read the syllabus.
- **Timetables can change** before the semester starts. Check the official timetable site before you
  enrol.

Found a bug or a wrong score? Open an issue on the repository.
